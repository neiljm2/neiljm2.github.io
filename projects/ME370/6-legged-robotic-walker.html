Project Overview

  • Course & Context: Design a functional walking robot for ME 370: Mechanical Design
  • My Role: Design the crank-slider dispensing mechanism, leg linkages, and ensure everything works together as a cohesive mechanical system
  • Objectives: Designing a walking robot that could...
  • Carry at least 5 payloads
  • Walk at a pace of 4m/min or greater without dropping payloads
  • Dispense one payload precisely every 2 meters
  • Do so without the use of traditional wheels with one motor operating continuously at constant speed.

    • Dispenser Mechanism Design

    The robot's dispensing function relies on a custom slider-crank dispenser mechanism coordinated with a compound spur gear train. The slider acts as a carriage for a single candy, moving between two positions. At one position, candy drops in from above, where it is then pushed above a hole for the candy to drop it. An anti-jam spring-loaded door ensures reliable dispensing without stalled motors. All motion is powered by a single motor; the spur gear train links the walking leg speed to the dispenser crank timing, guaranteeing exactly one candy is dropped every 2 meters of forward travel.

    Slider-crank dispenser mechanism.

    Puzzle Piece Chassis Assembly

    To maintain structural rigidity and allow for precise alignment, the main chassis was designed using a unique puzzle piece construction method. The structural plates were laser-cut from acrylic with interlocking tabs. These plates were securely fastened together using custom 3D printed brackets fitted with brass heat-set inserts. This combination of laser-cut puzzle tabs and threaded inserts provided an extremely strong, easily assembled framework that could withstand the dynamic stresses of the walking motion.

    CAD Assembly showing components connecting together.

    Leg Linkage Synthesis

    Rather than relying on known walking patterns like the Chebyshev, Lambda, or Strandbeest mechanisms, we experimented in Planar Mechanism Kinematic Simulator (PMKS) to synthesize a custom crank-rocker linkage with a foot path that fit our liking. This allowed us to specifically define a coupler path that featured a nearly flat bottom portion of the cycle, maximizing stable ground contact and ensuring optimal clearance during the return phase.

    PMKS Linkage Analysis

    Walking & Dispensing Performance

    The chosen 6-legged design organizes the legs into two groups of three, operating 180 degrees out of phase with each other. This setup guarantees that three legs (forming a stable triangular contact patch directly under the robot's center of mass) are always in contact with the floor. During physical testing, the robot walked steadily at approximately 6m/min, successfully dispensing the Starburst payloads at intervals averaging 1.95m.

    Walker traveling across the floor and dispensing a payload every 2 meters.

    Testing & Troubleshooting

    Initially, the robot exhibited a consistent "limp" and gradually veered off its intended straight path. Slow-motion analysis revealed that the leg joints were slipping slightly out of phase over time. This was caused by the set screws on the drive shafts not fully penetrating the heat sets, leading to loose joints. After redesigning the joints and implementing longer screws to secure the crank phasing, the unwanted turning behavior was eliminated.

    Final Theme & Integration

    To add a creative touch, the chassis was hand-painted to look like an octopus, with the six complex walking linkages visually representing crawling tentacles with two more tentacles painted onto the chassis. The main gear train was enclosed securely within the chassis, keeping the attention drawn to the smooth mechanical crawling motion.

    Final painted design walking in perfect synchronization.

    Outcome

    The final project was a huge success. The team met all structural, dimensional, and performance requirements, delivering a fully functional, reliable walking robot that dispensed candy on a strict mechanical timer. The iterative design process provided a fantastic hands-on experience in dealing with dynamic friction, linkage synthesis, and the realities of physical assembly tolerances. One of our final deliverables was a lighthearted team video showcasing our design process, so we got a little goofy with and made a parody of Temu ads we'd seen online. Enjoy :)
